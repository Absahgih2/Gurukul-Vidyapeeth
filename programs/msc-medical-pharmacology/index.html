Program Overview

The Master of Science (M.Sc.) in Medical Pharmacology is a specialised postgraduate programme designed to provide in-depth knowledge of drugs, their actions, therapeutic uses, side effects, and their impact on the human body. Pharmacology is a vital branch of medical science that bridges the gap between laboratory research and clinical healthcare practices. This programme focuses on understanding how medicines interact with biological systems, helping students develop expertise in drug discovery, experimental pharmacology, toxicology, clinical research, and pharmaceutical innovations.

During the programme, students gain a strong theoretical understanding along with practical laboratory exposure in areas such as molecular pharmacology, neuropharmacology, clinical pharmacology, chemotherapy, and pharmacokinetics. Graduates of this programme can explore opportunities in pharmaceutical companies, clinical research organisations, hospitals, academic institutions, drug regulatory bodies, and biomedical research centres.

Year-by-Year Syllabus

Year 1: Principles of Pharmacology, Pharmacokinetics & Pharmacodynamics

In-depth study of absorption, distribution, metabolism, excretion (ADME) of drugs, molecular mechanisms of drug actions, receptor theories, and dosage estimation. Lab work covers experimental setups, bio-assay methodologies, and dose-response curve records.

Year 2: Systemic Pharmacology, Chemotherapy & Toxicology

Detailed analysis of drug groups acting on central nervous, cardiovascular, renal, endocrine, and gastrointestinal systems. Core courses include antimicrobial chemotherapy, cancer chemotherapy, immunopharmacology, and biochemical toxicology of common toxins.

Year 3: Clinical Research, Pharmacovigilance & Dissertation

Comprehensive review of clinical trial phases, drug safety regulatory guidelines, adverse drug reaction (ADR) monitoring, and executing an original research project/thesis. Includes hands-on training with clinical database tools and research methods.
